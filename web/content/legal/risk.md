# Read this before you trade

DISCIPLE is a game, and the trades are real transactions on a blockchain. A blockchain is a public ledger. Robinhood Chain is the one these trades use. A rocket is a new coin. Buying is called BOOST. Selling is called DUMP.

The other pages are the [Terms of Use](/terms) and the [Privacy Policy](/privacy).

## You can lose everything you put in

Rockets are new coins. Many new coins lose value, and a rocket can fall until it is worth nothing. Only use money you can afford to lose completely. Nobody on the team will tell you what to buy.

## Prices move fast

The height on the screen is the rocket's market cap. Market cap is the price multiplied by the supply. That height can fall a long way in seconds. One large DUMP can drop the price hard. Your trade can land at a worse price than the price you saw when you tapped.

## The first market can work against you

Before a rocket moves to the shared pool, it trades on its own market. A BOOST pushes the price up. A DUMP pushes the price down. If you buy after other people, you pay a higher price. Someone who bought earlier can then sell to you at that higher price. A rocket may never grow enough to leave this market.

## The extra tax right after launch

In the first few seconds after a launch, every BOOST pays an extra tax that starts high and falls to nothing. If you buy in those first seconds, a large share of your buy can go to that tax. The launcher's own first buy does not pay it.

## The person who launched it can sell

Anyone can launch a rocket. We do not check that a rocket is a real project, or that its name and its story are true. A name or a picture does not mean a brand or a team stands behind it. We do screen uploaded pictures for sexual content. The launcher, and anyone holding a lot of the coin, can sell when the rules allow it.

A launcher can set a creator tax from 0% up to 7%. They choose it at launch, and it stays at that number.

The launcher picks a mode at launch, and it does not change. The modes are Easy, Hard, and Trench. Before the rocket moves to the shared pool, the mode limits how much one wallet can hold.

On Easy, each wallet can hold up to 1% of the supply. That limit rises to 3.33% as the rocket climbs.

On Hard, each wallet can hold up to 3.33% of the supply. That limit rises to 7.77% as the rocket climbs.

On Trench, one wallet has no holding limit.

These limits make one wallet's sell smaller. They do not stop many wallets from selling at the same time. After the rocket moves to the shared pool, these holding limits stop applying.

## What locked forever does, and what it does not do

When a rocket leaves its first market, its liquidity is locked forever in the Uniswap v4 LP. A liquidity pool (an LP) is the shared pile of tokens people trade against. Nobody can withdraw that pile. That lock does not hold the price up. Holders can still DUMP, and the price can still fall a long way.

## Contracts and the services around them

DISCIPLE depends on its contracts, on Robinhood Chain, on Uniswap v4, on the wallet inside the app or a wallet you connect, on the Alchemy account that wallet controls, on Privy for sign-in, and on a service that pays network fees in USDG. Any of these can have a bug, can be attacked, can stall, or can go offline. Funds can be lost, or they can get stuck. A confirmed transaction is final. We cannot reverse it.

## Fees add up

Every BOOST and every DUMP pays a 1% fee, plus any creator tax the launcher set. On an Easy rocket, while it is on its first market, a sell also pays a crew tip of 0.777% of the amount that sell would pay you, but only when the profit on that sale is above a threshold set for that rocket. Network fees are paid in USDG. Trading often costs more than a single trade suggests.
